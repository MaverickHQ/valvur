# Phase R49, the bar on maintained code: the record

Built on 2026-11-22 by the executor on the owner's Mac, with the image, the container tests,
the acceptance set and the Score on the AWS dev machine (D82t). It is the second phase of
[signal and adoption](../../.kiro/specs/signal-and-adoption/), stacked on R48's branch.

**The phase is not closed.** Seven of its eight tasks are done. The eighth re-bases the
baseline and holds two checks that are red on what was measured, and the spec says nothing
is bent to pass them: the owner decides (D82h). What was reached, and the decisions that
are the owner's, are below.

## What it built

- **R49.1:** measured first. Thirteen rules were under ADR-0033's bar on maintained code:
  five or more judged Findings on the corpus, and fewer than half true.
- **R49.2, R49.3:** the tier table, `src/valvur/data/tiers.toml`. A rule with a row is
  inventory, whichever Scanner's it is: its Findings stay in `findings.json`, say why in
  their title, are counted under *Listed for review*, and never make the verdict `findings`
  or fail the gate. `weak-hash` and the vendored `random` rule are listed everywhere.
- **R49.4:** a rule that matches a shape (a private key, a JWT, a generic high-entropy
  string, a curl header) is listed under a test, fixture, documentation or example path,
  and active everywhere else. A provider's own format has no row. Four more directory
  names are documentation.
- **R49.5:** `open-redirect` and `path-traversal` narrowed on what maintained code showed;
  `insecure-cookie` listed in tests.
- **R49.6:** two rules added, `valvur.javascript.insecure-cookie` and
  `valvur.python.tls-verification-off`. A third was written and is not shipped.
- **R49.7:** Opengrep's `--taint-intrafile` measured again and not adopted; a coverage note
  where a model is called and a sink is named with no flow reported between them.

## What is the owner's to decide

1. **The target is not reached.** D82h set 33 with at most 8 false active Findings. The
   track is at 29.4 with 12. Nothing was bent to reach it: no rule was demoted on one or
   two labels, and no label was changed. Closing the gap from here means deciding rule by
   rule on single Findings, which D82h says is not a measurement.
2. **Two of zizmor's rules are under the bar on R48's labels,** `dangerous-triggers` (5 true
   of 12) and `template-injection` (0 of 20). Demoting either takes 5 points from the
   infrastructure track, past the ratchet, and they are not valvur's rules to narrow. They
   are active. The choices: re-base that track (95.0 to 85.0 for both), leave them, or
   overrule the labels, which are the executor's.
3. **The JavaScript TLS rule is not shipped:** 0 true of 307 on maintained code, in the
   tests and benchmark scripts of HTTP libraries. It could ship listed in test material if
   `benchmark/` and `benchmarks/` were test material; D82f did not decide that.
4. **`--taint-intrafile` is not adopted,** by Requirement 6.2's condition: it catches none
   of the five advisories. It does catch all 34 generated flows, 30 without it, at no cost
   in time, and adds five unjudged Findings on the corpus from the request-data rules.
5. **A reading in R49.5:** a narrowed rule left with one or two false Findings is treated
   as *unmeasured there*, not as under the bar. The other reading lists `open-redirect`,
   `path-traversal` and `ssrf` everywhere, at 4.3 and 2.5 points of the Python track.
6. **R48's 241 verdicts and this phase's two** are the executor's, to be read and overruled.

## Two things the build's own checks caught

- **The acceptance set caught a new rule on valvur itself.** `6-self` failed on
  `ssl._create_unverified_context() if insecure else None` in valvur's client for an
  operator's internal mirror. The Python TLS rule now passes over a call chosen by a
  conditional expression.
- **The corpus caught the note saying something false.** With the rules' shared sources
  alone it told Flask and Litestar that they call a model: Click's test runner has an
  `invoke`. The mark now needs the file to import a model SDK, and the note is in the two
  corpus projects that do call one.

## What waits on those decisions

cases, the real-code track raised, and the README's numbers with it. Decisions 1 and 2 can
each move a track, so the re-base is held for them and not written twice. Until then the
committed baseline is R48's, every track is within its ratchet, and the README states
R48's numbers, which are lower than what is measured here and not higher. The same holds
for `docs/HOW-IT-WORKS.md`, which still says the request-data rules fire 23 times on the
corpus: they fire 5 times now, and that sentence moves with the baseline.

Two of R49.8's six behaviours are done: this record, and the scorecard, which now prints
the count of false active Findings beside the track. The other four are the re-base, the
test of the target, the test that the tier table and the measurement agree, and the
README.
